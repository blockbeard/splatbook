# Appendix B: Alchemy

> [!epigraph]
> “That night the King walked late in his chamber in the Iron Tower alone. His jars and flasks and bottles of blue and green and purple glass wherein he kept his cursed drugs and electuaries of secret composition, his athals and athanors, his crucibles, his horsebellied retorts and alembics and bainsmaries, stood arow on shelves coated with dust and hung about with the dull spider's weavings... The King was deep in his contemplation, with a big black book open before him on the six-sided reading-stand: the damnablest of all his books, the same which had taught him aforetime what he must do when by the wicked power of enchantment he had wanted but a little to have confounded Demonland and all the lords thereof in death and ruin.”
>
> _– The Worm Ouroboros, E. R. Eddison_

Alchemists are thieves. They are thieves of the worst sort.

**They steal souls**.

By butchering out the gallbladder, the liver, the bufonite, the alchemist distills the essence of a creature. This can only be done in death, of course, because death releases the soul. Distilling these reagents creates an alchemically reactive substance.

This allows the alchemist to use the powers of monsters. At least, for a while.

You can see why this is a vicious cycle.

# The Alchemist’s Tools

> [!lede]
> There are two essential tools that the alchemist requires: an **alchemy kit** and **hermetic bottles**.

An alchemy kit is a luxurious item that takes up 2 slots in your pack. It is a laboratory distilled down to its essentials: athanors, retorts, and alembics all wrapped up in a compact traveling case. An alchemy kit is needed for the **Brew Alchemy** Camp Action (see facing page).

Hermetic bottles are common items that each take up one slot in your pack. Hermetic bottles seal tight, keeping both **reagents** and **alchemical substances** safe and potent.

You must immediately brew each alchemical reagent you harvest into a substance or seal it in a hermetic bottle for preservation.

In essence, the number of reagents/substances you can carry equals the number of empty hermetic bottles you have. (See [[09 - Chapter 9 - The City Phase#The Omphalic Market|the Omphalic Market]], for details on equipment.)

## Reagents

Those strange creatures that dwell in the Underworld are alchemically potent. Their guts are, anyway.

Adventurers with the **Alchemy** talent know which viscera have alchemical properties and can **spend a watch** to harvest these **reagents**.

When you harvest a reagent and store it in a hermetic bottle, write down the reagent and the type of the creature it was harvested from like this:

- Hermetic bottle: Eyes of salamander

- Hermetic bottle: Toes of dire bat

- Hermetic bottle: Tongue of hellhound

Usually, any given creature can only produce **one** viable reagent. Huge monsters (elephant-sized or larger) might produce several. The GM will tell you what kind of reagents and how many can be harvested from a corpse.

Reagents can only be harvested from freshly killed creatures. Creatures that have sat for a watch or more lose their alchemical properties to rot and spoilage.

Similarly, harvested reagents that are stored outside of a hermetic environment cannot be used to brew alchemical substances. They rot too quickly.

### What can be harvested?

Creatures in the Underworld are inherently magical. It is this magic that alchemists exploit. **Only monstrous creatures provide reagents**.

Some monsters yield no reagents. A wraith, for example, has no **guts**. A magically animated statue has no guts. No guts = no **reagents**. The GM makes the call on edge cases (“Is the blood on a bloodybones alchemically reactive?”), but in general this will be obvious to everyone.

Sentient humanoids like humans, orcs, elves, or underfolk _never_ provide reagents.

## Brew Alchemy

Anyone with the Alchemy talent and an alchemy kit can Brew Alchemy as a Camp Action.

When you brew alchemy, you can turn reagents into **alchemical substances**.

- You transform as many reagents at the same time as you want.

- Each reagent is directly transformed into one alchemical substance.

- You choose how to transform each reagent. Eyes of salamander can be turned into a bomb at the same time as you turn the toes of dire bat into a potion and the tongue of hellhound into an oil.

At the end of the brew alchemy action, the alchemical substance replaces the reagent in the hermetic bottle. Erase the reagent and write the substance’s name like this:

- Hermetic bottle: Salamander bomb

- Hermetic bottle: Dire bat potion

- Hermetic bottle: Oil of hellhound

# Alchemical Substances
> [!sidebar] Forcing a potion down someone’s throat
>
> It is _really_ hard to get someone to drink something they don’t want to. A potion is probably about the size of a glass of wine. Imagine holding one of your friend’s mouth open and trying to get them to swallow a glass of wine. Can it be done? In the right conditions. Can it be done easily? No.

> [!lede]
> There are three types of alchemical substances.

**Potions (P)** are beneficial or transformational when drunk. A sip of a potion is enough to produce the tiniest effect that might hint at the bottle’s contents. Drinking the entire bottle gives you the full effect.

**Bombs (B)** are thrown in combat, exploding into a reactive spume of smoke and chemicals. This destroys the hermetic bottle. Bombs are almost always harmful or debilitating. Each bomb only affects a single target. During a Challenge, bomb effects are usually removed with the Recover action.

**Oils (O)** have an effect on the environment. They can be poured out, slathered onto items, or have things dipped into them to create these effects.

During Challenges, the **Use Item** Cups action is required to use any alchemical substance, whether that’s drinking a potion, throwing a bomb, or applying an oil ([[07 - Chapter 7 - The Challenge Phase#Cups actions|Cups actions]]).

## Alchemical effects

Alchemy bundles up a monster’s abilities and sticks them into a bottle. When you use an alchemical substance, you’re using a monster’s ability.

Drink a potion to gain a dire spider’s wall climbing ability.

Throw a bomb to poison a foe with the spider’s venom.

Distill the spider’s webbing into a glue-like oil that bonds together whatever it touches.

### Who does alchemy affect?

Potions only affect creatures with living processes. If you have blood, if you breathe, if you eat food, potions affect you. Creatures like the undead and automatons can neither benefit from nor be harmed by potions.

Bombs are usually chemically reactive with the air around them, creating fires, acid, webs, vines, etc. These substances affect both creatures and the environment, unless the GM makes a call otherwise.

Oils are chemical compounds that can make what they touch sticky, slippery, hot, invisible, etc. These substances affect whatever they touch, be it living or unliving, unless the GM makes a call otherwise.

## Creating alchemical effects

It is the GM’s job to determine what alchemical effects each creature can confer.

Not every monster produces potions _and_ oils _and_ bombs. Sometimes a creature might only be able to produce a potion. Tell players this up front when they harvest a creature’s reagents.

For creatures included in Appendix C, the GM’s task is relatively straightforward. We’ve included example substances for these monsters below.

For creatures of the GM’s own invention, a little thought and inventiveness is needed.

### Some advice for GMs:

#### Steal the creature’s powers

When an adventurer harvests reagents, look at the creature’s stats and choose a few **greater dooms** that seem relevant and easy to convert for an adventurer’s benefit.

- If the creature has an essential gimmick, it makes sense to include that gimmick as a potion. **Example:** A mermaid’s spleen can yield a potion of waterbreathing.

- Potions might also provide a meaningful defense against a monster’s essential gimmick. **Example:** A medusa’s snakes can yield a potion that makes the drinker immune to petrification for a watch.

- Does the monster have a signature attack? Seems like a good mechanic to tie to a bomb. **Example:** A dragon’s gallstone can yield a bomb that creates a fiery blast with the Critical effect.

#### Alchemy only lasts for a watch

Generally, a substance’s effect only lasts for a watch—no longer. Unless an effect is minor or restores a natural state, it will eventually wear off.

#### Disregard balance, embrace creativity

“Encounter balance” is not a meaningful concept in _His Majesty the Worm._

- If the players manage to defeat a strong creature, part of the reward is to gain strong alchemy from its corpse.

- Because alchemical substances are single-use items, powerful effects are a fun lark and have little longterm effect on a game.

#### Keep good ideas, discard the rest

When inventing alchemical effects, write out all of your ideas. Sift through them. Delete the ones that don’t seem to work. Keep the ones that do.

- Some monsters will produce identical effects. That’s okay.

- Some monsters will produce almost identical effects but a little bit better. That’s okay too.

### What do players know about alchemy?

The GM should not tell players what alchemical effects can be obtained from a monster until they brew alchemy. The mystery is part of the fun.

Can a player read this chapter and get a sense of the alchemical substances derived from the creatures in Appendix C? Of course. They can read a book. We’re not cops.

Reading this book still does not reveal the alchemical effects for monsters that the GM invents themselves.

There are two exceptions:

- **Bidding lore:** If an adventurer has a pertinent motif (e.g., Big Nerd Alchemist or Wizened Dragon Eater), they may bid lore to ask about a particular monster’s alchemical substances.

Each lore bid is a single question. That is, each lore bid reveals a single way that a reagent may be brewed: what are the effects of this monster’s potion, what are the effects of this monster’s bomb, etc.

- **Rumor mill:** Discovery is fun, pretending that you _don’t_ know something isn’t. The **rumor mill** ([[06 - Chapter 6 - The Crawl Phase|Chapter 6: The Crawl Phase]]) ensures that players don’t have to spin their wheels discovering something twice. If a guild learns about the alchemical effects of X, Y, and Z, the rumor mill allows them act on that information in-character in the future. Even in this case, though, the _GM_ isn’t the one revealing this information. The adventurers simply have a justification for knowing what they know outof-character. The players are responsible for tracking and remembering this information.

# A Sampling of Alchemical Substances

> [!lede]
> A list of example substances for the monsters in Appendix C can be found below. Effects mentioned here, like Rooted and Controlled, are defined see [[01 - Chapter 1 - The Basics#Effects|Effects]].

### Brain spider

**Potions (P):When** you speak, your brain literally hums and vibrates with psionic radiance. Anybody who hears you can **understand your speech—even** if you don’t share a language—because the meaning of your words is psychically broadcast.

**Bomb (B):** The bomb explodes into a silver web that covers the victim, **Rooting** them. This silver web also affects incorporeal creatures.

**Oil (O):** The chemicals in the brain spider’s spinnerets form a powerful glue. For a watch, this glue **sticks two things inseparably together**.

### Cockatrice

**Bomb (B):** Cockatrices devour their petrified victims using a special stone-dissolving gland in their throat. Distill this gland to create this bomb. The bomb creates a blast of smoke that has one of the following effects depending on the target:

- Destroys an item made of stone roughly the size of a person.

- Melts a hole in a larger stone item, such as a stone door.

- Deals **Critical damage** to a creature made of stone like a gargoyle or animate statue.

**Oil (O):Whatever** stone this oil touches becomes fleshy. Pouring the oil onto a petrified creature permanently **cures the Curse of petrification**. It also makes stone walls gross.

### Devil

**Potions (P):** You are now **immune to fire** for a watch. Your gear is still affected normally.

**Bomb (B):** The victim is **Controlled** to confess their gravest sin.

**Oil (O):** This oil burns with an **invisible fire**. It casts no light but generates heat, like an ethanol fire in daylight.

- This fire acts like a normal fire, consuming flammable items of wood, cloth, paper, etc.

- If you **dip** a metal weapon (like a sword) into it, it burns as hot as a torch but sheds no light. Your weapon suffers a Notch, but your attacks might be especially deadly to creatures who are weak to fire.

- The fire is not quenched by water. It can only be quenched by smothering.

### Face rat

**Potions (P):** An illusion covers you that makes you **look like a duplicate of the first creature you see** after drinking this potion. This illusion lasts for a watch.

- Illusions are purely visual, like a hologram.

- Creatures that are not affected by illusions can see through this effect.

**Bomb (B):** A face rat’s saliva rapidly grows dermis, which is used to create this bomb. The target’s ears, eyes, and mouth are immediately covered in skin: they are **Blinded, Deafened**, and **Silenced**. Each effect must be Recovered from separately; one Recover cures Blind, one Recover action cures Deafen, etc.

### Fungoid

**Potion (P):** You are immediately **Healed**. Tiny mushrooms sprout on your skin where your wounds once were.

**Bomb (B):** The bomb explodes into a choking cloud of spores. Assuming the target breathes, they cough so badly they are **Exhausted**. This effect lasts until the victim performs a Recover action.

**Oil (O):** A thick patch of huge mushrooms sprout wherever the oil is poured. The top card of the minor arcana discard pile determines the type of mushrooms:

- : giant inky cap (edible, poisonous with alcohol)

- : giant destroying angel (very poisonous)

- : giant hen of the woods (edible)

- : giant shrieker (magical, screams like an air raid siren)

### Griffin

**Potion (P):** The lion is the king of beasts but the griffin is the king of birds. When you drink this potion, a dungeon bird will seek you out, arriving in a watch or so. The dungeon bird will tell you a rumor.

- These are similar to the rumors you hear in the City ([[09 - Chapter 9 - The City Phase#^city-events|City Events]]).

- Dungeon birds look like owls but are not owls. The owls are not what they seem.

**Oil (O):** The tear ducts of the griffin are harvested to create this oil. It **cleans** anything it touches. Rust, minor flaws, impurities, and filth slide off and vanish. This can be used to take an impromptu “dungeon bath.”

### Harpy

**Potion (P):** Your **arms become wings** for a watch. You can **fly but** can’t hold anything. You cannot hover and must continue flying to stay in the air.

**Bomb (B):** The target suffers the **Inspire distaste** in its victim. The victim **hates** the **first creature** they see. Lasts for a watch. Creatures that do not feel emotions are not affected by this alchemy.

### Imp

**Potion (P):** You vomit and are **cured of any poisons** or **alchemical effects**. During Challenges, you are also Exhausted until you take the Recover action.

**Bomb (B):** You don’t want to know what part of the imp is used to create this bomb. The target is covered in a terrible stink. They are **Stunned** as they choke and cough. If they are an adventurer, they are also **Stressed** until they can wash off.

**Oil (O):** Whatever this oil touches becomes **utterly frictionless**. If poured out, it creates a slippery puddle with a 10’ diameter.

### Jinn

**Potion (P):** This potion is made from the jinn’s smokeless fire. When you drink it, you become **Shrouded**. You can also see **all Shrouded creatures**. However, this effect ends as soon as you interact with a visible object.

- A Shrouded creature is impossible to see without the aid of magic or special senses. Typically, if a Shrouded creature stands still and stays quiet, they cannot be deliberately targeted by creatures that can’t see them.

- If the Shrouded creature moves, those nearby may have a vague sense of their presence. All attempts to target them are made with disfavor.

**Bomb (B):** Derived from the eyes of the jinn, this bomb explicitly affects **intangible** creatures. Intangible creatures are forced into the material realm, making them both **visible and tangible**.

**Oil (O):** When poured onto the ground, this oil opens a **fiery hole in reality**. Anyone that enters it is teleported to the City, rocketing out of a manhole. A City Phase begins. This portal is open for about one minute.

### Kelpie

**Potion (P):** Gills etch themselves on your neck, like the Mariner from _Waterworld._ You can **breathe underwater** for a watch.

**Oil (O):** The kelpie walks on water using a secretion in its hooves. Whatever this oil touches **rejects water** for a watch.

- If poured onto water, this potion forms an island of oil about 10’ in diameter—the island is soft but sturdy enough to be used as a platform.

- If an object is covered in the oil, it will not sink no matter what—it can’t even be pushed forcibly under water.

- If you pour this on your boots, you can walk on water for a watch.

### Mimic

**Potion (P):** You can **talk to objects** for the next **three minutes** in real time (use a stopwatch).

- Objects know a lot about the tasks they were created to perform.

- Spears regularly quote Sun Tzu, mugs can tell you a liquid’s alcohol content, etc.

- Objects know a lot about what they’ve done.

- Swords know who they’ve killed; doors have a good description of people who have opened them, etc.

- Objects usually don’t know about a lot else.

- If you give the GM a beer, the object is particularly talkative. You can talk to the object for as long as the GM is drinking your beer.

**Oil (O):** If smeared on a non-living object, that object instantly **becomes a mimic**. The mimic is not loyal to you in any way.

### Nymph

**Potion (P):** You become **extremely beautiful**. Tests of fate to favorably influence the Disposition of creatures that would be attracted to you are made with favor ([[01 - Chapter 1 - The Basics#Example test of fate with favor|Example test of fate with favor]]). You also **cannot be Inspired or Controlled**. These effects last for a watch.

**Bomb (B):** The victim **falls in love** with the **first creature** they see. This is as though they have been **Inspired with joy**, except it’s romantic. This effect lasts for a watch. Creatures that do not feel emotions are not affected by this alchemy.

### Ogre

**Potion (P):** You become **immune to poisons** for a watch. In fact, nothing you eat or swallow hurts you at all. You can swallow live coals, the One Ring, or shards of glass to no ill effect.

**Bomb (B):** The victim emits a terrible, rage-inducing pheromone. **All creatures** in the same zone as the target are **Inspired with anger towards the target**.

- In combat, anger makes people stupid and clumsy. The angry creature will try and focus on the source of their anger. An angry creature will attack recklessly, with little regard to the environment.

- Creatures that do not feel emotions are not affected by this bomb.

**Oil (O):** The ogre can famously digest anything. Their digestive acids can be distilled into a **universal solvent**. This oil can break down any adhesive and makes materials soft and pliable: stone becomes mud, metal becomes bendable, wood becomes paper.

### Questing beast

**Potion (P):** You can **ask the** GM about the **location** of any one **person, place, or thing**. The GM will answer your adventurer with a sudden flash of prophetic insight.

**Oil (O):** Whatever the oil touches begins to **emit** a **sound** like the barking of many dogs. Draw twice on the [[06 - Chapter 6 - The Crawl Phase#Meatgrinder|Meatgrinder]] table. Any encounters drawn have noticed the sound and are headed to this area to check it out. This effect lasts for a watch.

### Slime

**Potion (P):** Your bones stop being bones and become fluid-filled sacs for a watch. You **take no damage** from **falls** for the duration. You can also **squeeze your body** through any space you can reach your hand through during this time, e.g., through a portcullis or into a mouse hole.

**Bomb (B):** The bomb explodes in a spray of neon-green acid ooze that covers the target. Any **armor the target** is **wearing is Destroyed**.

**Oil (O):** Slime oil is a powerful **acid** that can eat through any non-magical material except for glass, creating a hole about 5’ in diameter. It will eat through the floor.

- **Destroys** any non-magical object it touches.

- Deals **Critical damage** if it comes into contact with flesh.

### Titan

**Potion (P):** Your body quickly swells and you suddenly **Grow**. This effect lasts for a watch.

- When this massive size would be advantageous to tests of fate (e.g., contests of strength, lifting gates), gain favor. When this would be disadvantageous to tests of fate (e.g., squeezing into holes, sneaking around), gain disfavor.

- Your massive size does not provide favor to Challenge Actions.

**Oil (O):** Whatever the oil is smeared on becomes **ten times as heavy**. This doesn’t create any structural problems for the object itself (statues don’t collapse under their own weight, for instance) but can create structural problems for the environment around it.

### Ungoat

**Potion (P):** You **cannot be targeted with spells** (as if you’re wearing iron armor) for a watch. You also cannot cast spells.

**Bomb (B):** The target immediately draws on a random [[11 - Appendix A - Sorcery#Maleficence|Maleficence]] table. Whatever effect occurs is centered on the target.

**Oil (O):** The oil **negates any active spells** affecting the thing it's smeared on for a watch.

- This can pause permanent enchantments on objects for a watch (e.g., the flaming sword is just a regular sword).

- This instantly ends active spells that sorcerers are concentrating on.

### Vampire

**Potion (P):** You **transform** into a thick **mist**. Your equipment drops to the ground. This lasts for a watch.

- You can move at a walking pace in your mist form.

- You can move freely through small cracks, under doors, through keyholes, etc.

- Most attacks and environmental hazards cannot harm you. The GM can make a ruling if certain circumstances (e.g., strong winds, super cold temperatures) provide any problems.

**Bomb (B):** The target **suffers** all of the [[13 - Appendix C - Dungeon Denizens#Vampire|vampire]]’s weaknesses for a watch. They become Stunned whenever they are exposed to sunlight, wholesome herbs (e.g., garlic, wolfsbane, or wild rose), silver, or running water.

### Winter wolf

**Potion (P):** You become **immune to cold temperatures** and **ice-based damage** for a watch. You can stand outside in a blizzard in your small clothes comfortably.

**Oil (O):** As soon as the oil touches air, it begins to **freeze**.

- Will **freeze a body of water** the size of an Olympic swimming pool.

- If poured onto the ground, it forms a **wall of ice** 10’ tall, 10’ wide, 1’ thick. Walls of ice are opaque and impermeable.
